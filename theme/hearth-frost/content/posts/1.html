Most of what keeps a built-in refrigerator cold happens out of sight. The condenser is a set of coils and fins that releases the heat removed from inside the cabinet. On most built-in Sub-Zero models it sits at the top of the unit, behind a grille. On under-counter models it is usually behind the kick plate at the bottom.

Over time, the condenser collects dust, lint and pet hair. That layer acts like a blanket. Heat cannot escape, so the compressor runs longer and harder to do the same work.

Why it matters

  • Stable temperatures. A clogged condenser is one of the most common reasons a refrigerator starts running warm.
  • Less wear. Longer run times put extra strain on the compressor and fan motors.
  • Lower energy use. A clean condenser lets the system cycle as designed.
  • Quieter operation. A unit that is not overworking is a unit you rarely hear.

How to clean it

Check your owner's guide for the exact location of the condenser and grille on your model. Then:

  1. Turn off the power. Use the unit's control or, better, switch off the circuit breaker. Many built-in units have a power switch behind the grille as well.
  2. Remove the grille. Most grilles lift or tilt out. Do not force them.
  3. Brush gently. Use a soft condenser brush and work in the direction of the fins, top to bottom. Do not use metal tools. Bent fins restrict airflow.
  4. Vacuum the loose dust with a brush attachment. Avoid pressing on the coils.
  5. Replace the grille and restore power. Give the unit a few hours to settle before judging its temperature.

The fins can be sharp. Wear gloves, and keep your hands clear of the condenser fan.

How often

Every six months is a good rhythm for most kitchens. If you have pets, live near the coast or have had recent construction in the home, check it every three months.

When to call a technician

Cleaning solves a dirty condenser. It will not fix a failing fan, a refrigerant leak or a faulty control. Call us if:

  • the unit still runs warm a day after cleaning;
  • the compressor runs constantly or cycles on and off rapidly;
  • you hear grinding, rattling or loud clicking from the top of the unit;
  • you see oil residue on the coils or tubing;
  • you cannot reach the condenser safely.
